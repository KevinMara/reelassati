### Application dates relevant to this product

- Article 4 AI-literacy duties have applied since 2 February 2025.
  Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 amended the operative wording to require providers
  and deployers to take measures supporting the development of AI literacy,
  taking relevant contextual factors into account. It does not create a single
  mandatory course, certificate, or guaranteed individual competence level.
- Obligations for providers of newly placed general-purpose AI models have
  applied since 2 August 2025. Models placed on the market before that date have
  the applicable transition until 2 August 2027.
- Article 50 transparency duties apply from 2 August 2026.
- The Article 50 transition introduced in 2026 is narrow: systems already
  placed on the market or put into service before 2 August 2026 have until
  2 December 2026 for the Article 50(2) marking/detection duty. It does not
  create a comparable grace period for Article 50(1) or Article 50(4).
- New Article 5(1), points (ba) and (bb), and paragraphs 1a and 1b concerning
  non-consensual intimate synthetic content and child-sexual-abuse material
  apply from 2 December 2026. REELassati blocks those uses now as a product
  boundary and rights/safety control, without misdescribing the later AI Act
  application date.
- The 2026 amendment delayed Annex III high-risk rules to 2 December 2027 and
  Annex I product-system rules to 2 August 2028. See the [Commission entry-into-force notice](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-omnibus-enters-force).

The final, non-binding Article 50 guidelines interpret the obligations as not
requiring retroactive labelling of outputs generated before 2 August 2026 merely
because they remain stored, while encouraging voluntary transparency. Treat
that as Commission guidance rather than a separate statutory exemption.

## Mandatory official template with limited scope

The [Commission GPAI training-content summary template](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/template-general-purpose-ai-model-providers-summarise-their-training-content)
is mandatory for a provider of a GPAI model subject to Article 53(1)(d). It does
not apply merely because REELassati calls an upstream model through an API. If
REELassati trains, places on the market, or materially modifies a GPAI model,
`GOV-001` requires immediate reclassification and this template must be
reassessed.

## Official materials still pending or technically unsettled

- As of the final Article 50 guidelines and this 4 August 2026 review,
  standardised, provider-independent, interoperable detection solutions and
  relevant harmonised technical standards had not yet fully emerged. Recheck
  the official position before every material release. No single watermark,
  provenance format, C2PA implementation, or vendor solution can honestly be
  described as the legally mandated or “EU-certified” method.
- Pending standards do not suspend Article 50. Until suitable standards exist,
  a provider may use its own or shared third-party solution if it can
  demonstrate effectiveness, reliability, robustness, interoperability,
  technical feasibility, and an appropriate detection path.
- EU AI Board recommendations/common objectives for Article 4 AI literacy and
  further Commission examples remain developing. Existing examples do not
  create a presumption of compliance.
- The final Article 50 guidelines note that joint Commission–EDPB guidance on
  the relationship between the AI Act and EU data-protection law is under
  preparation. Data-protection review must therefore remain a separate gate.

## Interpretation rules for this repository

1. Distinguish the legal rule from the Commission's recommended implementation.
2. Never convert “may,” “should,” or a Code commitment into a statutory “must.”
3. Never treat an exception as automatic. Preserve the facts and evidence that
   make it applicable.
4. A substantive human editorial review can affect only the Article 50(4)
   public-interest-text disclosure exception. It does not automatically remove
   Article 50(2) machine-marking duties or deepfake disclosure.
5. Creative/artistic/satirical/fictional deepfakes are not exempt. Their
   disclosure may be presented proportionately so it preserves enjoyment and
   normal exploitation of the work.
6. A technical mark hidden from humans cannot satisfy a required human-visible
   disclosure; a visible label alone cannot satisfy Article 50(2)'s technical
   marking and detection requirements.
7. Provider responsibility is not discharged merely because an upstream model
   or platform supplied a mark, model card, contractual promise, or detector.

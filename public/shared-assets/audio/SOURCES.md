# Bundled editor audio

Original collection verified 15 September 2026; uiSFX addition recorded 25 October 2026. The current active catalog contains 35 sound effects and five music tracks (40 entries); every entry is CC0-1.0. This includes eight original Zen UI cues. Cinematic Percussion remains a bundled legacy file but is not an active catalog entry. Older public URLs are preserved so existing projects are not broken.

Source pages were checked for the exact uploaded files, not inferred from their availability or from a general site license. Optional creator credits are retained in the catalog and audio metadata.

Audio is downloaded and bundled, not hotlinked. The original collection's effects are 16-bit PCM WAV and music is 192 kbps MP3. Those files retain their source duration and channel count, use at least a 44.1 kHz source, and receive only constant gain attenuation where necessary to provide headroom. See manifest.json for their original and bundled SHA256 hashes and modifications, and technical-validation.json for duration, sample rate, channels and measured levels after a complete FFmpeg decode.

The eight uiSFX additions (`achievement`, `check`, `checkpoint`, `close`, `collapse`, `complete`, `connect`, `copy`) are original MP3s, unchanged from the pinned upstream revision above. Their source paths, SHA256 hashes, measured durations and waveforms are recorded separately in `contracts/editor-audio-zen.ts`. The upstream audio-specific CC0 notice is preserved in `uisfx-zen/LICENSE-AUDIO.txt`; it applies to the audio files and is not inferred from the repository's software license.

Selection is based on relevance, the creator's descriptions, actual reusable files, permissive rights and technical verification. No claim that every item is viral, famous or human-listened is made. The sounds have preview controls so editors can audition them. No asset license charge or AI credits apply to these bundled files; ordinary hosting, bandwidth and account-storage expenses still exist.

The Wilhelm entry is the complete 39.275-second original recording session, including multiple takes and vintage tape/room noise; it is not falsely presented as a single isolated scream. The exact 48 kHz/24-bit source is independently labeled CC0 by Craig Smith on Freesound and in the Internet Archive mirror https://archive.org/details/SSE_Library_VOICES . The bundled copy is converted to 16-bit PCM WAV with constant attenuation, as recorded in the manifest.
